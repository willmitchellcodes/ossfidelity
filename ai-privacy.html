How to read this

Every answer gets one of three marks: protective by default, protective once you change a setting, or not protective. "Not disclosed" counts as not protective, because trust requires verification. We don't boil this down to a single grade. The two useful numbers are how many protections are already on, and how many you can turn on yourself. The gap between them is the setting work the company leaves to you.

Red flags mark problems no setting fixes:

  • Chats can become public with one tap, or shared links are being found by search engines
  • Chats read by human reviewers are kept more than a year after you delete them
  • There's no self-serve way to opt out of training

A past incident that has since been fixed, like shared links once showing up in Google, keeps a sharing answer at "not protective" but isn't a red flag.

This covers consumer accounts only. A school or work account (for example, Microsoft 365 Copilot through your university) may have very different terms, so check with your IT office.
